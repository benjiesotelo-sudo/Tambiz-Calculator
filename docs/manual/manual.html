Before you start

Tambiz is the awarding night of MGT1114 Business Plan 2. Groups defend their business plan and run a booth, a panel of judges scores them, and every student's grade comes out of those scores. The Tambiz app does the scoring, the ranking and the grading. It asks for one workbook in and hands you two files out.

Read this first

This edition replaces every earlier one. The app was cut back in September 2026 to seven coordinator tabs and two judge screens. The class roll, the adviser list, the groups and members pages, the students' and advisers' private links, the release screen and group codes are all gone. If you have an earlier printed copy, recycle it: its pictures show screens that no longer exist.

The Tambiz Manual | Chapter 1

How Tambiz thinks

Seven ideas. Once they are clear, every screen makes sense.

One workbook inStudents with their group and adviser, and the judges. You upload it on the Data tab.
›
Judges scoreEach on their own phone, group by group, in the Defense or the Booth.
›
You close the eventWhen every group is judged, nobody can change a score.
›
Two files outThe workbook for the department, and the email file that sends each student and adviser their result.

1.1 An event is one year

Each year's Tambiz is one event, for example Tambiz 2028. Its students, groups, advisers, judges and scores all belong to it and to nothing else, so last year can never leak into this year's grades. Past events stay in the list, readable, for as long as the app exists. The app also comes with a practice event, full of invented people, for trying things out.

1.2 Groups, students and advisers

A group is a business, known only by its name, for example KAPE KULTURA. There are no group numbers. Each student belongs to one group. Each group has one adviser; an adviser may have several groups. All three come from the same rows of your workbook: the groups are the different names in its Group column, the advisers the names in its Adviser column.

Group names are compared ignoring capitals, spaces and punctuation, so Kape Kultura and KAPE KULTURA are the same group.

1.3 Judges

A judge signs in with their own email and password, on their own phone. The app makes the password and shows it to you once, on a sign-in slip to print. Because every judge signs in as themselves, the app knows who gave every score, which is what makes corrections and judge profiles possible. A judge's account is kept from year to year.

1.4 Two halves, seven categories

Every group is judged twice: in the Defense and at the Booth. Each half has its own categories, and each category a list of criteria with a maximum for each.

HalfWeightCategories (criteria · points)
Defense70%Elevator Pitch (5 · 100) · Infomercial (4 · 40) · Paper (9 · 100) · Product Demo (3 · 100). Defense judges also score every student: Presentation /20, Communication /40, Q&A /40.
Booth30%Best Business Plan Booth (8 · 100) · Student Innovation (4 · 100) · Best Product Demonstration (6 · 100)

39 criteria in all: 21 in the Defense, 18 at the Booth. The points, the 70/30 weights and the letter bands are fixed; only the wording of each criterion can be changed on screen (chapter 3).

1.5 A sheet, and what "submitted" means

One judge's scores for one group in one half is a sheet. A judge fills it in and then taps Mark group complete. That sheet is now submitted.

Read this twice

Only submitted sheets count. A sheet a judge started and never marked complete counts for nothing: not in any percentage, rank, grade, file or judge profile. The Progress tab shows these as in progress · not counted so you can chase them.

1.6 A blank is never a zero

A criterion nobody scored is left out, not counted as zero. A group that is not fully scored reads Incomplete and gets no rank, so a half-judged group never looks like a weak one. The single exception is a student marked absent from the defense: their own individual scores count as zero (chapter 4).

1.7 Closing, and the point of no return

When judging is over you close the event. Judges can no longer change a score, and the two files are ready. You can still undo closing, and you can still correct a score with a reason, until you download the email file. From that moment nothing about the event can change, because the emails would no longer match.

The event saysWhat it means
Open for judgingJudges on the event can score. You can change anything.
ClosedJudges cannot change scores. You can correct a score with a reason, download the workbook, or undo closing.
Closed, email file downloadedFinal. Nothing can change. You can download both files again.

What the app does with each mistake, if you upload it anyway

FlagWhat the upload does
BLANKSkips the row and says so, for example "Students row 12 has no Email, so it was skipped."
TWICEKeeps only the later of the two rows, and says so. The student on the earlier row is left out until you fix the number and upload again.
CHECK EMAILSkips the row: "Students row 6: “kristine.aquino.example.edu.ph” is not an email address, so the row was skipped."
ADVISER DIFFERSRefuses the whole group and names the rows (chapter 3, step 10). Everybody else is uploaded.

Read this twice: two mistakes the app cannot see

A wrong email and a wrong section upload silently. The app checks only that an email looks like one (something@something.something). andrea.reyez@example.edu.ph looks fine, uploads fine, and sends Andrea's grade to nobody, or to somebody else. A section written Sec 2 instead of Sec - 2 becomes a section of its own. Formulas cannot catch a real-looking wrong address: have a second person read the Email column against the class roll before you upload, and fix any mistake later directly in the Data table (chapter 3).

The Tambiz Manual | Chapter 4

How a score becomes a grade

One student, Andrea Reyes of KAPE KULTURA, followed from the judges' scores to her letter grade. Every number is from the Tambiz 2028 pictures in this manual.

Criterion averagesEach criterion: the average of the judges who submitted.
›
Category %Averages added up, over the maximums added up.
›
Half %The categories of the half, averaged.
›
Group overallDefense × 0.7 + Booth × 0.3.
›
Final grade(Member total + group overall) ÷ 2, rounded up, then its letter.

4.1 The group's score

Step A · One category: Elevator Pitch

Two defense judges submitted a sheet for KAPE KULTURA: Dr. Liza Manalo and Mr. Paolo Dizon. For each criterion the app takes the average of their two scores.

CriterionMaxDr. ManaloMr. DizonAverage
1. The problem is stated clearly…20171616.50
2. The customer and market…20181617.00
3. The product answers the problem…201816.517.25
4. The pitch closes with a clear ask2016 (corrected)16.516.25
5. Delivery is confident…2016.51716.75
Elevator Pitch10083.75 → 83.75%

Averages added up: 83.75 out of 100 points, so 83.75%. A category's percentage uses only criteria that have at least one score, so a blank criterion is left out rather than counted as zero.

Step B · The two halves

Defense = (Elevator Pitch 83.75 + Infomercial 81.25 + Paper 81.50 + Product Demo 83.25) ÷ 4 = 82.44%
Booth   = (Best Business Plan Booth 85.50 + Student Innovation 88.50 + Best Product Demonstration 89.00) ÷ 3 = 87.67%

Every category weighs the same within its half. Infomercial is out of 40 points, not 100, but its percentage counts the same as the others'.

Step C · The group overall

Overall = Defense × 0.7 + Booth × 0.3 = 82.4375 × 0.7 + 87.6667 × 0.3 = 57.71 + 26.30 = 84.01%

The app carries the halves unrounded until the end; working with the two-decimal figures gives the same 84.01. Every student of KAPE KULTURA shares this number.

4.2 The student's own score

Step D · The member total

In the Defense, each judge also scores every student: Presentation /20, Communication /40, Q&A /40. For each of the three, the app averages the judges who scored it, then adds the three averages.

Andrea ReyesMaxDr. ManaloMr. DizonAverage
Presentation20181717.50
Communication403532.533.75
Q&A40343233.00
Member total10084.25

Until every one of the three has at least one judge's score, there is no member total and no grade; a partial total is never scaled up.

Step E · The final grade

Final grade = (Member total 84.25 + Group overall 84.01) ÷ 2 = 168.26 ÷ 2 = 84.13

Step F · Round up, then the FEU letter band

84.13 rounded up to a whole number = 85  →  85 is in 85–91  →  B+, 3.5 quality points

Read this twice

The grade is always rounded up. Andrea's 84.13 becomes 85, a B+; rounded the ordinary way it would have been 84, a B. Any fraction at all goes up: 84.01 becomes 85 too.

4.3 The rules that decide the numbers

RuleWhat it means
Only submitted sheets countA sheet the judge never marked complete is ignored everywhere. Mr. Dizon started HALO-HALO HUB's defense and never submitted it, so HALO-HALO HUB's defense rests on Dr. Manalo's sheet alone.
A blank is never a zeroA criterion, category or half nobody scored is left out. A group with no booth score at all has its defense as its overall.
Incomplete groups are not rankedUntil every criterion in both halves has a score on a submitted sheet, a group reads Incomplete, with no rank and no place on the leaderboard.
Two decimals, one roundingEvery percentage shows two decimals; the screens and the workbook round the same way, so they always agree.
Ties go to the higher overallIn Infomercial, SARI-SARI SMART and BANIG & CO. both show 78.13%. SARI-SARI SMART's overall (81.81) is higher than BANIG & CO.'s (78.60), so it ranks 3rd and BANIG & CO. 4th. Two groups share a place only if their overalls are equal too.
Absent means zeroA student marked absent from the defense has zero for every individual score nobody gave, and a grade worked out as usual (below).
Scores above the maximum are refusedNever quietly changed to the maximum. The judge's phone refuses them, and so does the server.

An absent student

Kristine Aquino of BANIG & CO. missed the defense and was marked absent. Her member total is 0, so her final grade is (0 + 78.60) ÷ 2 = 39.30, rounded up to 40: F. That is the department's rule, applied exactly. If the department decides otherwise for a student, type their individual scores on the group's scores page with a reason (chapter 6); the grade, the workbook and the email follow.

Checking a grade by hand in Excel

With the member total in A1 and the group overall in B1:

Then read the letter from the band table above. For Andrea: A1 84.25, B1 84.01, C1 85, B+.

If it goes wrong

A failed build does not break the live site: Vercel keeps serving the last version that built. The change simply is not live. Tell whoever made it; the ✗ links to Vercel's log, which needs the Vercel sign-in.

Redeploying without a change

After changing a setting (9.4) the site must be rebuilt, because Vercel applies new values only to new deployments.

Confirm on first use

In Vercel: open the project, then Deployments. Open the newest deployment marked Production and use its Redeploy button. Wait until it shows Ready, about a minute.

Rules for whoever changes the code

Give this list to any programmer you ask for help. It comes from the repository's own notes (CLAUDE.md, README.md).

  • Never edit index.html. It is the original calculator and the authority on the scoring rules; the tests check the app against it.
  • Every scoring change keeps npm test passing. The rules, and where the department's decisions deliberately differ from the old calculator, are in src/lib/scoring.ts.
  • Database changes only add, never remove or rename, because the live database may receive them before the code that uses them.
  • Never commit real student data. The repository is public. Excel and CSV files are blocked on purpose.
  • Never run a copy of the app against the live database without the coordinator's say-so. A copy on a laptop uses its own local database unless told otherwise.
  • The app is deliberately small: no new screens, files or sign-ins without the course owner's say-so.

Upkeep that cannot be skipped

The app is built on Next.js, whose makers publish security fixes, and Vercel retires old build tools. A site not rebuilt for a year may fail to build just when a change is needed. So once a year, a month before the event, a programmer updates it, the checks pass, and it is merged while there is time to fix anything (9.6). It needs Node.js 22 or newer to build.

This manual lives in the same repository, in docs/manual/, with a README saying how to print it again after a change.

Sending the emailsThe app writes them; Power Automate sends them (chapter 8). The Power Automate screens are not pictured in this manual.
Checking that an email address is the right oneOnly its shape is checked. A second person reads the Email column (chapter 2).
Frozen results and file historyClosing locks the scores, but results are worked out afresh each time; the saved workbook is the snapshot (9.8).
A screen for the change historyScore corrections are listed on each group's scores page and in the workbook. Other changes (uploads, moves, closing) are recorded in the database with no screen to read them.
Corrections after the email fileNothing can change once it is downloaded, and there is no "send corrected results" step (9.7).
Sign-ins for students or advisersDeliberately none. Each gets one email.
